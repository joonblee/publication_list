\begin{pubentry}{P1}{Searching for New Physics Inside Jets with the Herwig 7 Generalised Parton Shower}
\pubauthors{Taehee Kim, \pubme\corr, M. R. Masouminia, Michael H. Seymour and Un-ki Yang.}
\pubdetails{Accepted for publication in \textit{JHEP} (2026). \arxivlink{2604.00617}.}
\contributionitems{\checkitem{Study proposal}\checkitem{Analysis strategy design}\checkitem{Theoretical calculations}\checkitem{MC production supervision}\checkitem{Sample analysis}\checkitem{Sensitivity studies}\checkitem{Figures \& numerical results}\checkitem{Manuscript writing}\checkitem{Referee responses}}
\contribution{I \keycontribution{conceived and independently led this study from proposal through peer review}, extending my BSM-shower development~\pubref{P2} into a phenomenological investigation of new physics inside jets. The central question was how a light $Z^{\prime}$ radiated during shower evolution would appear after decaying to muons, and which observables could distinguish it from Standard Model (SM) backgrounds.
\contributionbreak
Using a $U(1)_{B-L}$ benchmark with simultaneous SM and beyond the SM (BSM) showers, I \keycontribution{characterised non-isolated dimuon signatures inside jets}, studied their correlations with the surrounding jet and evaluated prospective sensitivity. The study shows how shower radiation can populate regions poorly served by conventional isolated-lepton selections, and identifies discriminants for a dedicated search. It provides the phenomenological basis for my CMS dimuon-in-$b$-jet analysis\ifincludeinprogress~\pubref{R1}\fi\ and motivates extending this approach to other unconventional lepton and jet signatures.}
\end{pubentry}

\begin{pubentry}{P2}{Generalized angular-order parton showers in Herwig 7}
\pubauthors{\pubme\corr, M. R. Masouminia, Michael H. Seymour and Un-ki Yang.}
\pubdetails{\textit{JHEP} \textbf{08} (2024) 064. \arxivlink{2312.13125}; \doilink{10.1007/JHEP08(2024)064}.}
\contributionitems{\checkitem{Study proposal}\checkitem{Funding application}\checkitem{Theoretical calculations}\checkitem{Herwig 7 implementation}\checkitem{MC production}\checkitem{Physics validation}\checkitem{Phenomenological analysis}\checkitem{Figures \& numerical results}\checkitem{Manuscript writing}\checkitem{Referee responses}}
\contribution{Un-ki Yang's proposal for a dimuon-in-$b$-jet search\ifincludeinprogress~\pubref{R1}\fi\ raised a modelling question: how could new particles be produced inside a jet, and how could their production be simulated consistently with the accompanying SM processes? I \keycontribution{proposed the generalised BSM-shower project}, applied for and received a Marie Sk\l odowska-Curie Fellowship, and pursued the development under Michael H. Seymour's supervision. I \keycontribution{calculated the helicity-dependent splitting functions and implemented the algorithms in Herwig~7}, including automatic support for Universal FeynRules Output (UFO) model input.
\contributionbreak
The framework treats splittings involving scalar, fermion and vector states within a common formulation, with validation against fixed-order (FO) calculations. Its UFO interface makes the same shower machinery usable across different BSM models. This supplied the simulation needed for~\pubref{P1} and the CMS search, turning an experimental motivation into a reusable modelling capability. A \href{https://github.com/joonblee/herwig74alpha}{public development version} is available ahead of Herwig~7.4\ifincludeinprogress~\pubref{R3}\fi. This study advances our understanding of parton showers and provides a foundation for modelling light BSM particle radiation at future high-energy colliders, where large logarithmic enhancements can make multiple emissions significant and require resummation beyond FO calculations.}
\end{pubentry}

\begin{pubentry}{C1}{Implementation of the CMS-HIG-18-011 analysis in the MadAnalysis 5 framework (exotic Higgs decays via two pseudoscalars with two muons and two $b$-jets; 35.9~fb$^{-1}$)}
\pubauthors{\pubme\corr\ and Jehyun Lee.}
\pubdetails{\textit{Mod. Phys. Lett. A} \textbf{36} (2021) 2141003 (peer-reviewed conference proceeding).\\
\doilink{10.1142/S0217732321410030}.}
\contributionitems{\checkitem{MadAnalysis 5 implementation}\checkitem{Efficiency \& resolution modelling}\checkitem{MC production}\checkitem{Cut-flow validation}\checkitem{Statistical interpretation}\checkitem{Figures \& numerical results}\checkitem{Manuscript writing}}
\contribution{I selected this analysis at the MadAnalysis~5 school because its muon and $b$-jet final state offered a foundation for my planned non-isolated dimuon search\ifincludeinprogress~\pubref{R1}\fi. With guidance from the MadAnalysis~5 team, I reimplemented the CMS search for $h\to a_1a_1\to\mu^+\mu^-b\bar{b}$ and validated the selection against public CMS results. This required translating the published reconstruction and selection into a working recast, including the treatment of $b$-tagging efficiencies and mass resolutions. For the tested signal benchmarks, the resulting predictions reproduced the published CMS signal yields to within a few per cent.
\contributionbreak
This early study developed my understanding of muon and $b$-jet reconstruction and selection in CMS. It established the practical foundation for connecting generator predictions to experimental objects and for checking detector-response approximations against published CMS reference results. That experience informs my subsequent phenomenological studies~\pubref{P1} and CMS search design\ifincludeinprogress~\pubref{R1}\fi.}
\end{pubentry}

\begin{pubentry}{P3}{Analytical modelling of frequency splitting and modal degeneracy restoration in imperfect wine-glass shell resonators}
\pubauthors{\pubme.}
\pubdetails{\textit{Int. J. Eng. Sci.} \textbf{227} (2026) 104593. \doilink{10.1016/j.ijengsci.2026.104593}.}
\contributionitems{\checkitem{Study proposal}\checkitem{Analytical modelling}\checkitem{Geometrical balancing}\checkitem{Numerical calculations}\checkitem{COMSOL validation}\checkitem{Figures \& numerical results}\checkitem{Manuscript writing}\checkitem{Referee responses}}
\contribution{Drawing on my background in aerospace structures and vibration, I initiated this study by asking whether high-$Q$ shell resonators could provide a basis for new-physics probes beyond conventional collider reach. A practical obstacle is that small structural imperfections split vibration modes that would otherwise have equal frequencies; compensating this splitting can require costly tuning equipment or additional control hardware. I \keycontribution{developed a geometrical method to restore equal frequencies within mode pairs} (modal degeneracy) through a suitable combination of added and removed mass.
\contributionbreak
As sole author, I formulated the mechanical model, evaluated the effects of imperfections and the compensation conditions, and validated the predictions with numerical calculations and COMSOL. The key result is that the relevant dynamical symmetry can be recovered without restoring the shell's complete geometrical symmetry. This geometrical approach offers a route to reducing the cost and hardware demands of frequency tuning. I am now considering how these mechanical results could be translated into practical BSM-sensing concepts, including possible dark-matter applications.}
\end{pubentry}

\begin{pubentry}{R1}{Search for anomalous dimuon resonances within b jets in proton-proton collisions at $\sqrt{s}=13$ and 13.6~TeV}
\pubauthors{CMS Collaboration --- internal analysis; analysis contact: \pubme.}
\pubdetails{\textbf{CMS-NPS-26-009}. \npsstatus}
\contributionitems{\checkitem{Analysis strategy design}\checkitem{Signal modelling}\checkitem{MC production supervision}\checkitem{Analysis software}\checkitem{Background estimation}\checkitem{Systematic uncertainties}\checkitem{Statistical inference}\checkitem{Figures \& numerical results}\checkitem{Analysis Note}\checkitem{Manuscript writing}\checkitem{Internal review}}
\contribution{I \keycontribution{lead the search and perform the full analysis}, targeting an underexplored CMS phase-space region with \keycontribution{non-isolated dimuon resonances inside $b$ jets}. The physics aim is to test light new states in a region where nearby hadronic activity can make conventional isolated-muon selections inefficient. The central experimental challenge is to retain signal acceptance while controlling large SM backgrounds. I developed the analysis around data-driven background estimates and a consistent treatment of detector and modelling uncertainties, so that this unconventional signature can be tested quantitatively in collision data.
\contributionbreak
As analysis contact, I lead the scientific presentation in the Analysis Note and manuscript and coordinate the CMS internal review and its responses. The search brings my BSM-shower development~\pubref{P2} and the phenomenological study I initiated~\pubref{P1} into one experimental programme. It has required me to connect theoretical calculations, generator development, signature characterisation and experimental decisions within a coherent search strategy. I aim to extend this approach to other unconventional lepton and jet signatures, using the underlying physics to guide the modelling and analysis choices.}
\end{pubentry}

\begin{pubentry}{R2}{Modelling physics processes with MadGraph5\_aMCatNLO and parton shower Monte Carlos in the CMS experiment}
\pubauthors{CMS Collaboration --- internal manuscript; analysis contact: \pubme.}
\pubdetails{\textbf{CMS-GEN-25-002}. \genstatus}
\contributionitems{\checkitem{Study proposal}\checkitem{MG5 v3 integration in CMSSW}\checkitem{MG5 v3 validation}\checkitem{Validation team leadership}\checkitem{MC production}\checkitem{Validation workflows}\checkitem{Figures \& numerical results}\checkitem{Manuscript writing}\checkitem{Internal review}}
\contribution{I \keycontribution{proposed the paper's scope} to make CMS's practical experience in event modelling available as a reference for other analyses and future collider studies. The central objectives are (1) documenting and assessing the event-generation strategies and physics-modelling settings used in CMS across Run 2 and Run 3, including MadGraph5 configurations and parton-shower tune parameters, (2) examining the roles of MadGraph5 and other generators in modelling the main physics processes simulated by CMS, and (3) evaluating MadGraph5\_aMC@NLO version~3 and hardware acceleration through physics validation and end-to-end performance benchmarks within CMS production workflows, in preparation for the HL-LHC. To address these objectives, I \keycontribution{lead the MG5 version~3 validation team as a CMS generator validation group convener}, defining validation strategies, coordinating team contributions and working with generator developers to investigate discrepancies in physics predictions and resolve technical issues in CMS integration.
\contributionbreak
I integrated \mgfive\ version~3 into CMSSW and developed comparison workflows for the team's validation across SM processes, including matching and merging. The paper also summarises how CMS develops its simulation workflows to meet the computational demands and precision requirements of the HL-LHC. This work complements my BSM-shower development by applying my generator expertise to modelling and validation across the CMS collaboration, providing a broader foundation for simulation studies at the HL-LHC and future colliders.}
\end{pubentry}
